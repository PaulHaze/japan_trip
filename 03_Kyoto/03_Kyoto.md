### MUST DO'S!

**CLAUDE'S TOP 5 MUST VISITS**

1. **Fushimi Inari at dawn** (train — Keihan from Gion-Shijō, 10 min). Arrive 06:30–06:45, before sunrise (~07:00 in early Dec). The torii tunnels are lit and near-empty. Do the full summit loop past Yotsutsuji — most people turn back there and the upper mountain is a different, quieter place. 4.5 km, ~2 hours round trip. Best photography: the first hour after sunrise when low light filters through the gates. Pair with the Fushimi sake district in the afternoon (Keihan south to Chūshojima, 8 min) — Torisei yakitori brewery for dinner to close it out. A full day, but it's one train line the whole time.

2. **Shimogamo Shrine & Tadasu no Mori** (bikeable — 20 min up the Kamo River path, flat and traffic-free). The single strongest recommendation for your dates. Tadasu no Mori is a fragment of primeval forest whose maples are late-peaking — early-to-mid December — so you'll catch greens through oranges to reds in a single canopy. Almost no tourists compared to the marquee temples. Best light: morning, sun slanting through the trees. Combine with the Botanical Gardens and Ichijōji (all in a line heading north-east).

3. **Tōfuku-ji at opening, 08:30** (short train from Kyoto Station or ~25 min bike). Early December is exactly when the Tsūten-kyō bridge maples peak. Get there at opening — by 10am it's shoulder-to-shoulder. Don't skip the Mirei Shigemori Hōjō gardens: modernist checkerboard moss, one of the great 20th-century gardens in Japan, and always emptier than the bridge. The neighbouring Sennyū-ji (Imperial mortuary temple) is almost never visited and worth 30 minutes.

4. **Daitoku-ji complex** (bikeable — ~25 min from hotel). Not one temple but a walled monastic town with two dozen sub-temples. Kōtō-in's bamboo-and-maple approach is one of Kyoto's great autumn sights. Zuihō-in and Daisen-in for dry-landscape gardens. And **Hōshun-in Bonsai Garden** (appointment only) is the best bonsai viewing in Kyoto — essential for the wife. Lunch at Izusen (shōjin ryōri in the temple garden) or Ichimonjiya Wasuke (aburi-mochi since roughly the year 1000). Go in the morning for the best light on Kōtō-in's maples.

5. **Arashiyama — the quiet end** (train day — San-in Line from Kyoto Station). Skip the bamboo grove crush and head uphill: Saga-Toriimoto (preserved thatched-roof street), Otagi Nenbutsu-ji (1,200 unique stone rakan — almost nobody goes), Giō-ji (exquisite moss garden in late autumn), Ōkōchi Sansō (best view in Arashiyama, the entry fee keeps it calm). Walk down to Togetsukyō bridge for sunset (~16:45) from the south side. Pairs naturally with the Kameoka sword forge day — Kameoka is one stop up the same line.

**SOL'S TOP 5 MUST VISITS**

Five alternatives to Claude's picks, researched 17 November 2026. Suggested visit lengths and walking times are planning estimates. These are options to choose between, rather than five extra days to squeeze in; autumn colour depends on the season.

1. **Murin-an — my strongest garden pick** (roughly 30–35 min walk east from the hotel). A villa garden built around flowing water, open lawn and the borrowed scenery of the Higashiyama hills. For someone interested in bonsai, this offers a different look at how Japanese gardeners compose trees and landscape at full scale. Allow 60–90 minutes, including time to sit with tea. Pair with Nanzen-ji below for one relaxed half-day. Use the official site's visit-reservation link for your date. [Official garden and reservations](https://murin-an.jp/en/).

2. **Nanzen-ji's Suirokaku aqueduct and the canal landscape** (walk onward from Murin-an, or subway to Keage). The brick arches bring a completely different photographic subject to a temple itinerary: repeating geometry, water infrastructure and wooded surroundings. I'd allow 1–2 hours for the precinct and a short canal-area walk, choosing any paid temple interiors separately. Particularly convenient to combine with the existing Tozando sword-gallery suggestion in the wider Okazaki area. [Kyoto City's canal walking guide](https://biwakososui-museum.city.kyoto.lg.jp/en/walk/01/).

3. **Nijō Castle, prioritising Ninomaru Palace** (subway from Sanjō Keihan to Nijōjō-mae). My choice for adding the political world of the shoguns to your wife's sword interests. Give the palace and gardens 2–3 hours; select a ticket that includes Ninomaru, since admission options differ. **Avoid Tuesday 1 December: Ninomaru Palace closes on Tuesdays in December.** Monday 30 November or Wednesday 2 December would suit better. Honmaru is a separate booking decision. [Official opening and closure information](https://nijo-jocastle.city.kyoto.lg.jp/) · [Official tickets](https://nijo-jocastle.city.kyoto.lg.jp/admission/ticket/?lang=en).

4. **Uji — Byōdō-in, its museum and a proper tea stop** (Keihan from nearby Sanjō, changing at Chūshojima for Uji). My preferred easy excursion if you'd like a change from central Kyoto. Make the Phoenix Hall and Hōshōkan museum the anchor, then walk by the river and stop for tea. Allow a generous half-day; don't turn it into a race through Fushimi Inari and all the sake breweries as well. The museum's cloud-riding bodhisattvas are a reason to go beyond the familiar exterior photograph. [Official Byōdō-in guide](https://www.byodoin.or.jp/common/pdf/pamphlet-en.pdf) · [Official rail access](https://www.byodoin.or.jp/common/pdf/en/access-train.pdf) · [Nakamura Tokichi's Byōdō-in tea shop](https://global.tokichi.jp/pages/byodoin-store).

5. **Ōhara and Sanzen-in — the countryside option** (subway to Kokusaikaikan, then bus to Ōhara and an uphill walk). Moss, ancient cedars and a village setting make this the biggest change of pace in my list. Choose it if you want a slower outing and can spare most of a day including travel. Go for the garden atmosphere rather than relying on December maple colour. Sanzen-in publishes December hours of **09:00–16:30**, adult entry **¥700**, and a roughly 23-minute bus journey from Kokusaikaikan followed by a 10-minute uphill walk. [Official visitor information](https://www.sanzenin.or.jp/en/).

**My priorities:** Murin-an + Nanzen-ji together, then Nijō Castle. Pick Uji for tea and art, or Ōhara for a rural day.

### Michelin quality lunch spots

**CLAUDE'S SUGGESTIONS**

You said one special lunch, budget tight otherwise. All of these are walking distance from the hotel (Sanjo/Kawaramachi). Go for lunch — same kitchens as dinner, often half the price.

1. **Kappo Sakamoto** (Gion, 1 Michelin star) — Already locked in on your restaurant list and rightly so. Six counter seats, ~¥5,000 for a six-course lunch, chef cooking in front of you, English spoken. This is the one. 15 min walk from hotel. Book early — six seats fill fast.

2. **Wagokoro Izumi** (Karasuma/Shijō, held 2 Michelin stars for 14 consecutive years) — Eight-dish omakase lunch from ~¥12,000 (A$105–125). Dashi-led, the chef personally meets his producers. 5 min walk from Shijō. The best pedigree-to-price ratio in the city at lunch. Closed Mondays. Cash only.

3. **Restaurant Hiramatsu Kōdaiji** (Higashiyama, 1 star for 7 consecutive years) — French technique on Kyoto ingredients, lunch from ~¥6,500 (A$58). Four-storey building with a sweeping view over Yasaka Pagoda. The lunch is absurd value against the dinner price. Closed Mondays. ~20 min walk or short bike from hotel.

4. **Kyoto Wakuden** (JR Kyoto Isetan 11F) — The 156-year-old house's station outpost. ¥7,000 Suoh course, tax and service included, is genuine Wakuden cooking for A$62. No booking anxiety. Also works as an arrival-night dinner (Sun 29, you'll be at Kyoto Station at 17:00 anyway) if you want to save the special lunch for later in the week.

My pick for the one big lunch: **Wagokoro Izumi**. Fourteen years at two stars, A$125 all-in, walking distance, and you'll eat as well as anywhere in the city at twice the price.

### Bonsai Stuff for the wife

### Traditional raked Zen gardens — wife's must-do

These are **karesansui**, or dry-landscape gardens: gravel/sand raked into patterns around larger stones. Given this specific interest, prioritise one of these above Murin-an, which offers a different kind of garden. Public admission lets you view the gardens; it does not guarantee seeing a gardener rake them.

1. **Ryōan-ji — the classic choice.** Fifteen rocks arranged in a rectangular expanse of raked white gravel, viewed from the temple veranda. This is my first pick for the iconic traditional rock-garden experience. Allow about an hour for the garden and grounds; aim for opening, checking seasonal hours before travelling. In northwest Kyoto, so plan it as a dedicated outing rather than a quick stop from Sanjō. The temple website was unavailable during this check, so exact current fees/hours are not confirmed here. [Official Kansai tourism guide](https://www.the-kansai-guide.com/en/directory/item/12306/).

2. **Daisen-in, Daitoku-ji — best fit with the bonsai day.** A historic dry landscape with stones forming a symbolic mountain-and-water scene, alongside raked gravel. Claude already mentions it, but this new preference makes it a priority rather than an optional sub-temple. Combine with Hōshun-in if its visiting arrangements work out. The temple's visitor sheet lists **¥500**, **09:00–17:00 March–November**, **09:00–16:30 December–February**, open year-round. **Photography is prohibited.** Allow 45–60 minutes. [Japan National Tourism Organization](https://www.japan.travel/en/spot/1157/) · [Temple visitor information](https://daisen-in.net/information.pdf).

3. **Kennin-ji — convenient central alternative.** A Zen temple with a dry-landscape garden in the Gion area, approximately 20–25 minutes' walk from your hotel. A good option for an unhurried hour looking at the gardens and temple art during a Higashiyama outing. [Japan National Tourism Organization](https://www.japan.travel/en/spot/1182/).

**SOL's recommendation:** Make **Ryōan-ji** a must-do, and include **Daisen-in** on the Daitoku-ji/bonsai day if you want two different interpretations. Visit lengths and walking times above are estimates; sources checked 17 November 2026.

### Bonsai visits

1. **Hōshun-in Bonsai Garden, Daitoku-ji** — The main event. Opened 2021, designed by Seiji Morimae, displaying museum-grade bonsai including *kichō bonsai* (formally registered masterpiece trees) in a proper Kyoto garden setting. The best bonsai viewing in Kyoto by a wide margin. **Appointment only — book now.** Inside the Daitoku-ji complex, so pair with Kōtō-in and the other sub-temples.

2. **Kyoto Botanical Gardens — standing bonsai collection** — A large outdoor display of pines, maples and wisteria, free with garden entry (¥200). Not Hōshun-in's level but a good broad collection. On the Kamo River path, bikeable. Pairs with the Shimogamo/Ichijōji day.

3. **Nippon Bonsai Taikan-ten** — One of Japan's largest annual bonsai exhibitions, held in Kyoto in late November. Recent years ran roughly 21–25 November, which would be just before you arrive — but dates shift yearly. **Check the 2026 schedule.** If it happens to extend to 29–30 Nov, reroute for it; it's a bigger deal than anything else bonsai-related in the city.

4. **Wakuden Daitokuji Shop** — The Wakuden group's small shop right by Hōshun-in. Open 10:00–15:30, closed Thursdays. Worth a stop after the bonsai garden for beautifully packaged Kyoto sweets and gifts.

### Sword stuff for the wife

1. **Masahiro Tantōjō sword forge, Kameoka** — The centrepiece. A licensed, practising swordsmith ~40 min west of Kyoto. Over ~4 hours you work *tamahagane* (the same steel used for real katana) and forge a 15 cm kogatana blade, which you keep. The smith teaches the history and metallurgy as you go. ¥38,000pp. Runs Tue/Thu/Sat/Sun — Thursday 3 Dec, 09:00 session. **Book well ahead** — small operation, limited places. San-in Line to Chiyokawa, then bus.

2. **Kitano Tenmangu Treasure House** — Autumn special exhibition "Tenjin-sama and the Toyotomi Family" runs 31 Oct – 6 Dec 2026, covering your dates. Displays the Kunihiro tachi (donated by Toyotomi Hideyori) and a Sukemori tachi, both Important Cultural Properties. ¥1,000 admission, 45–60 min. 10:00–16:30 (last entry 16:00). Bikeable from the hotel (~20 min). The best historical sword viewing available in Kyoto during your stay — the Kyoto National Museum is closed for reinstallation 30 Nov – 21 Dec.

3. **Gallery Tozando + Tozando Shogoin** — Gallery Tozando specialises in fine Japanese swords by modern master swordsmiths, displayed for sale. The sister shop Tozando Shogoin (near Heian Shrine) carries antique swords, armour and martial-arts equipment. Commercial galleries rather than a museum, but the quality is real. 11:00–18:00 at Shogoin. Confirm Gallery hours separately.

4. **Genkō-an** (north-west Kyoto) — A temple with the famous round "window of enlightenment" and square "window of delusion" framing a maple garden. Also has a blood-stained ceiling made from the floorboards of Fushimi Castle, where samurai committed seppuku in 1600. The boards were installed as a memorial. Bikeable but on the edge of town — pair with Kōetsu-ji nearby (bamboo-fenced approach, one of the most photographed paths in Kyoto among locals).

5. **Teradaya, Fushimi** — The inn where Sakamoto Ryōma was ambushed in 1866 and escaped through a window with a sword wound. Preserved with the marks in the woodwork. 3 min from the Fushimi breweries, so it slots naturally into the Fushimi sake day.
