## Monday 23 — Meiji Jingu, Harajuku and HATO

**Default choice: keep the harvest ceremony and the cat-café idea.** This is the date-specific experience already accepted in the consolidated brief.

Aim to reach a shrine entrance around **08:45–09:00**, allowing time for the forest approach before reaching the main precinct around 09:30. Enter from Yoyogi for the north-side walk, or Harajuku for the simpler south approach. There is no need for the earlier plan's 07:00 departure unless you actively want a long photography morning.

**10:00: Niiname-sai**, the harvest thanksgiving rite. The shrine's official calendar lists **23 November at 10:00**. This is a religious observance; follow staff instructions about viewing and photography, and don't assume access to the inner ceremony. [Meiji Jingu rites](https://www.meijijingu.or.jp/en/whattosee/rites/).

**Late morning–afternoon:** walk out toward Harajuku, have lunch, then visit a cat café if still appealing. Choose the café and check its session/booking rules ahead of time. Takeshita Street can be a short look; use the smaller surrounding streets or Omotesando if the holiday crowds become tiring. Today is Labour Thanksgiving Day, so allow extra patience around Harajuku. [Official Japanese holiday calendar](https://www8.cao.go.jp/chosei/shukujitsu/gaiyou.html).

**Evening:** **HATO in Hatsudai**, if you want to retain that special dinner. Book a suitable seating rather than assuming walk-in space. The official site lists irregular closures and shorter Sunday/holiday dinner hours. [HATO reservations and hours](https://restaurant-hato.com/).

I would **not** also add Togoshi-Ginza, Musashi-Koyama and a bath between Harajuku and Hatsudai. Keep those as local fallbacks if you decide to shorten Thursday's shopping.

**Two alternatives:**

- **Antiques win:** replace the shrine/Harajuku day with **Oedo Antique Market at Tokyo International Forum, Yurakucho**. The organiser currently lists **Monday 23 November, 09:00–16:00**, cancelled in rain. Go in the morning, browse properly, have lunch nearby and keep the afternoon loose. - **Crowds win:** after the shrine, skip Takeshita Street and the café; have a quiet lunch and a hotel rest before dinner.

## Wednesday 25 — swords, hotel break, then Shibuya → Sangubashi → Shinjuku

**Morning: the sword museum. Afternoon: recharge at the hotel. Evening: walk north from Shibuya around the west side of Yoyogi Park, with sake and neighbourhood stops, finishing in Shinjuku's neon.**

### 09:30–12:30 — compact Ryogoku morning

Leave Gotanda around **08:30**, allowing roughly **50–60 minutes** to reach the **Japanese Sword Museum**. Yamanote to Akihabara, then the local Sobu Line to Ryogoku, is one straightforward route; check the actual service on the day.

- **09:30–11:00:** Sword Museum, near opening. Give the blades proper attention.
- **11:00–11:20:** optional short walk through neighbouring **Kyu-Yasuda Garden**.
- **Afterwards:** early lunch nearby if convenient, then head back. Aim to reach Gotanda around **12:30–13:00**.

The museum currently lists **Master Swordsmiths of East and West: Kotetsu and Sukehiro, 24 October–20 December 2026**, **09:30–17:00, last entry 16:30**. Its holiday closure rule makes Tuesday 24 unsuitable, while Wednesday fits. [Official exhibition information](https://www.touken.or.jp/museum/exhibition/exhibition.html).

Keep the rest of Ryogoku for another trip: adding stable practice or a second museum would eat into the break you specifically want.

### 13:00–15:30 — back to Gotanda and chill

A real hotel break: put your feet up, charge phones, change layers and leave museum purchases behind. Have lunch here if you didn't eat in Ryogoku. Depart when refreshed, aiming to reach **Shibuya around 16:00** on the Yamanote Line.

### Around 16:00–21:00 — Shibuya to Shinjuku via the west side of the park

**Walking route:** Shibuya Station → **Koen-dori / Udagawacho → Kamiyamacho / Tomigaya → Yoyogi-Hachiman / Yoyogi-koen station area → Sangubashi → Minami-Shinjuku → Shinjuku**.

This follows the **left/west side of Yoyogi Park**, through the neighbourhood streets you want. Use **Yoyogi-Hachiman and Sangubashi as map waypoints**: JR Yoyogi Station is on the east side and would pull you onto a different route. Stay on public streets around the park and shrine grounds so the evening doesn't depend on gates being open. The [Shibuya tourism neighbourhood map](https://play-shibuya.com/pds2024/wp-content/themes/play/pdf/map/guideMap_TomigayaUehara_en.pdf) is useful for the Tomigaya / Yoyogi-Hachiman section.

Allow approximately **5–6 km and 90 minutes to two hours of actual walking to Shinjuku**, depending on detours. This is a planning estimate; with food, sake and browsing, allow **four to five hours** before the Shinjuku finale. The additional neon loop comes afterwards.

| Loose window | Suggested flow |

**One concrete sake lead:** **Tokyo Sake BAL Anbai, Yoyogi-Hachiman** (東京酒BAL 塩梅), **1-3-6 Tomigaya**. Keep the second sake stop flexible around Sangubashi; check the menu, opening hours and space when you arrive. These stretches are quieter neighbourhoods, so expect pockets of places around the stations rather than continuous nightlife from Shibuya onward. Two relaxed food-and-sake stops give the walk its shape without turning it into a race between bars.

### Around 21:00 onward — finish in Shinjuku's neon

Suggested flow: **Omoide Yokocho → east-side neon → Kabukicho → Golden Gai**.

1. **Omoide Yokocho, west/northwest side of Shinjuku Station.** Lanterns, smoke, tight lanes and counters. Have a skewer or a drink if something appeals; having eaten along the walk means you don't have to queue for dinner here.
2. **Walk around the north side of the station toward the east-side streets.** Take in the illuminated signs and busy crossings en route to Kabukicho.
3. **Kabukicho:** enter through the red gate, wander **Ichibangai and the Godzilla Road area**, then the surrounding public streets and the **Tokyu Kabukicho Tower** area. This is the part for the neon, host-club signs, nightlife and slightly seedy atmosphere you described. Spend time walking rather than treating the gate as the whole visit. [JNTO's Kabukicho guide](https://www.japan.travel/en/spot/1668/).
4. **Golden Gai:** finish with its little lanes and a bar that looks welcoming to visitors. Check the cover charge and drink prices before sitting down. One good bar is plenty; stay longer if the conversation is good. [Golden Gai association](https://golden-gai.tokyo/en/).

The walking loop takes roughly **90 minutes to two hours without long bar stops**. With stops, it can comfortably become a midnight-or-later finish. Omoide Yokocho, Kabukicho and Golden Gai provide different experiences, so I would sample all three rather than substitute a club automatically.

For Kabukicho, choose entrances yourselves and ignore street touts; ask prices before ordering. Respect photography restrictions, particularly in Golden Gai and around people working in the district.

**Getting home:** the Yamanote connects Shinjuku and Gotanda, but check the **actual last useful train that evening** if you want to use it. Save the hotel address and budget for a taxi if you decide to stay beyond trains. A taxi home is a reasonable way to keep the night open without an all-nighter.

**Two fallbacks:**

- **The walking starts to drag:** from Sangubashi, take the Odakyu Line to Shinjuku and keep the neon finish. You will already have done the west-side neighbourhood section.
- **Rain or a particularly good bar changes the pace:** linger for food and sake, then take a taxi to Shinjuku. Shorten the finale to Kabukicho and one alley district rather than forcing the full loop.

## Thursday 26 — slow morning → Nakano watches → Koenji afternoon

**Sleep in after Wednesday.** There is no reason to arrive in Nakano before the watch shops open, and the main outing begins around lunchtime.

### Around 12:00–15:30/16:00 — Nakano watches and browsing

Travel **Gotanda → Shinjuku on the Yamanote Line → Nakano on a suitable Chuo service**. Allow roughly **40–50 minutes to reach Broadway**, including the interchange and walk. From Nakano's north exit, follow **Sun Mall** to **Nakano Broadway**.

Make the watch dealers the anchor:

- **Jackroad / Bettyroad:** a useful starting point for comparing men's and women's watches. [Jackroad's official account lists daily opening from 11:00](https://page.line.me/xat.0000184114.wip).
- **Kamekichi:** nearby on Broadway's third floor, making comparison easy. Its **Nakano shop** currently lists **11:00–20:30 with no regular closing day**; the Wednesday/Thursday closures shown elsewhere on its site apply to other branches. [Kamekichi shop information](https://www.kame-kichi.com/foroverseas/en/shop.html).

Have lunch and browse collectibles, odd shops and second-hand finds between dealers. Bring your passport if a purchase is possible. Ask about condition, service history, warranty coverage and the final price before deciding. Kamekichi's current site flags a **new tax-free refund system from 1 November**; get the shop to explain the procedure applicable to your purchase rather than assuming the old checkout process. [Kamekichi's current visitor information](https://www.kame-kichi.com/foroverseas/en/shop.html).

**Aim to leave around 15:30–16:00**, but treat that as a reminder to look at the time. If a watch purchase is taking longer, shift the later windows rather than rushing it.

### Around 16:00–19:00 — Koenji shops and an early dinner

Nakano → Koenji is **one stop west on a stopping Chuo/Sobu service**. Check the stopping pattern before boarding.

Start south of the station around **PAL and Look shopping streets**, then follow the vintage clothes, record shops and side streets that appeal. Have a coffee break and drift toward the station-side lanes for an early dinner if hungry. [GO TOKYO's Koenji guide](https://www.gotokyo.org/en/destinations/western-tokyo/koenji/index.html).

The afternoon is deliberately split between the two places: watches first, Koenji afterwards. If a purchase takes longer than expected, accept a shorter Koenji browse. If watches are only a quick look, move across earlier and give Koenji more time.

**Return to Gotanda around 20:00–20:30** as a loose target, leaving a comfortable packing window before Friday's train. Koenji gets some early-evening atmosphere; Wednesday remains the late night.

**Two fallbacks:**

- **Still recovering:** reach Nakano later and concentrate on your shortlisted watch dealers, then decide how much Koenji you want. A brief wander and dinner there still works.
- **Not in a shopping mood:** shorten Nakano and give Koenji a café-and-neighbourhood afternoon. If you need to stay close to the hotel instead, **Togoshi-Ginza** is the local shopping-street alternative, two stops from Gotanda on the Ikegami Line.

## What I would leave out — and when to swap

There are only five full days after the hotel move. **Yanesen, Jindaiji, Kichijoji and Shibamata remain good ideas**, but fitting them all in would undermine the neighbourhood-based trip you prefer.

If you want one larger substitution, **Yanaka → Sendagi → Nezu** would be my first choice for a quiet old-Tokyo walk. It would require replacing part of a planned day, rather than squeezing it between Wednesday's museum and evening walk or Thursday's two shopping neighbourhoods. **Jindaiji** is the alternative for a temple-and-woodland day, but involves more travel and suits a fresh day better than the morning after Shinjuku. Check the chosen attractions' current opening arrangements before making either swap.

**Sumo practice is an opportunity, not an itinerary foundation.** The basho ending on 22 November does not guarantee Tokyo practice immediately afterwards. I would drop it from the default plan. If it matters enough, enquire about **Tuesday 24**, explicitly accepting a shorter Asakusa day, and book only on a confirmed stable schedule.
