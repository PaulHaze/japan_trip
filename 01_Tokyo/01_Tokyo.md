**HOPPY STREET SUGGESTIONS:**
About 10-15 open-air izakayas spilling onto the street, two blocks west of Senso-ji. Most are cash only. No reservations — just pick one that looks good and sit down. For a light lunch, stick to skewers and small plates rather than the stews:
- **Good light picks:** Yakitori skewers (¥100-300 each), edamame, karaage (fried chicken), gyoza. Easy to graze without getting weighed down.
- **Skip for light lunch:** Motsu nikomi (offal stew) — it's the signature Hoppy Street dish but heavy. Save that for a drinking session.
- **The drink:** Order a "Hoppy Set" — a mug of shochu with a bottle of Hoppy (a beer-like drink). It's the thing to do here. Around ¥500.
- **Budget:** Most dishes ¥300-700, drinks ¥400-600. A light lunch for two with a couple of drinks: ~¥3,000-4,000.
- **Timing note:** The street really comes alive in the evening (5pm+), but stalls do serve from lunchtime. Some stalls have irregular closing days — most should be open on a Tuesday but if a couple are shut just move to the next one.

**KNIFE SHOP SUGGESTIONS**
Kama-Asa is the main target but Kappabashi has 15+ knife shops. If you want to compare or browse:
- **Kama-Asa (釜浅商店):** Your pick. Founded 1908, 1,000+ knives, English-speaking staff, free name engraving. Open 11:00-17:30. They only stamp the maker's mark, not their own name — purist approach. At 2-24-1 Matsugaya (southern end of the street).
- **Kiwami (貴和美):** Worth a look if you want something high-end. Known for ZDP-189 steel blades (extremely hard, HRC 67-68). They let you test-cut in store. English-speaking staff, free engraving. Open 10:00-18:00. At 1-5-17 Nishiasakusa.
- **Washindo (和心堂):** Biggest selection on the street — 1,000+ knives from makers across Japan. Specialises in kurouchi (black-forged) blades which look incredible. Multilingual staff (English, Chinese, Korean). Open 10:00-18:00.
- **Hikari (日加利):** The one for beautiful handles — natural wood, stabilized wood, turquoise, deer antler. Also carries artisan knives and Kyoto natural whetstones. Open 10:00-18:00.
- **Tips:** Bring your passport for tax-free (over ¥5,500). Ask to hold and test-cut before buying — grip and balance are personal. Budget: ¥5,000-15,000 for a solid home knife, ¥15,000-40,000 for professional grade. Knives must go in checked luggage, never carry-on — shops will wrap for travel.

**SOL SUGGESTIONS**

**Strong agreement with the Nakano → Koenji pairing:** This is a good final Tokyo day. I would aim for Nakano around **11:30–12:00**, give the watches 2–3 hours, and reach Koenji mid-afternoon. Keep the walk between them optional: if you have spent hours standing in watch shops, taking the train buys more energy for the vintage shops.

**Watch buying — what I would actually do:**

- Make a shortlist of **two or three exact references and a maximum price** before Thursday. Compare Jack Road and Kame-Kichi for the same reference, condition and included accessories, rather than treating the cheapest example of a model as automatically the best buy.
- Ask about service history, polishing, replacement dial/hands, bracelet length and spare links, box/papers, and the written warranty. On a vintage watch, originality and condition may matter more than a small price difference. Try it on before committing.
- Ask what happens if a problem appears **after you return to Australia**: where repairs happen, who pays shipping, and whether the warranty is the dealer's or the manufacturer's. Jack Road publishes its own warranty terms; do not assume every watch has worldwide manufacturer cover. [Dealer warranty terms](https://www.jackroad.co.jp/img/global/pdf/warranty_trad_en.pdf).
- Keep time for bracelet sizing, payment and paperwork. Ask about the final price politely, but don't build the budget around a discount being available.

**Important update to the tax-free advice above:** Your visit is after Japan's **1 November 2026 switch to a refund system**. At participating shops you pay the tax-inclusive price, with the consumption-tax refund following customs confirmation that the goods leave Japan. Bring your original passport, ask the dealer how its refund is paid and whether fees apply, and compare the final cost after those fees and card conversion. Keep purchases available for customs inspection at departure. This also matters for Tuesday's knife purchase; ask about the inspection procedure before checking in luggage containing it. [Japan Customs explanation](https://www.customs.go.jp/kaigairyoko/taxrefund.htm).

**BAGGAGE FORWARDING (Takkyubin / Ta-Q-Bin)**

Send your bags on **Thursday 26 November** (the day before travel) so they arrive at your Matsumoto accommodation on Friday.

**How to do it:**
1. **Thursday afternoon/evening:** Ask the hotel front desk at JR-East Hotel Mets Gotanda to send your bags via Yamato Transport (クロネコヤマト — the black cat logo). Most Tokyo hotels do this routinely for guests.
2. **Fill out the slip:** The hotel staff will help. You need:
   - Destination: your Matsumoto hotel name, address, and phone number (have this written down)
   - Delivery date: Friday 27 November 2026
   - Your name and the hotel's address as sender
3. **Cost:** ~¥2,000-3,000 per standard suitcase (depends on size/weight). Pay at the hotel — cash or card usually accepted.
4. **Confirm with Matsumoto hotel:** Let them know bags are arriving on Friday via Yamato. Most hotels will hold them at reception for you.

**Alternative drop-off points:** Any 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, or Lawson can also send Yamato packages (look for the black cat sign), but the hotel is far easier as staff handle the paperwork.

**What to carry on Friday:** Just a day bag with essentials — travel documents, valuables, a change of clothes, and anything you'll need on the train. Your suitcases will be waiting at the Matsumoto hotel when you check in.

**Do NOT send bags on Friday morning** expecting same-day delivery — Tokyo to Matsumoto is next-day delivery on the standard service. Thursday send = Friday arrival.
