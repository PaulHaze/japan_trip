## 1. Tori-no-Ichi — Arrival Night (Thu 19 Nov)

**Status:** Your wife arrives Thu 19 Nov. The second (and final) Tori-no-Ichi falls on exactly that date.

- Ōtori Shrine and Chōkoku-ji, near Iriya / Minowa stations (Hibiya Line) — NOT at Asakusa Station
- ~100 kumade (lucky rake) stalls, ~800 food stalls, lanterns, free entry
- Best window: 15:00–17:00 for lantern atmosphere without the 19:00–23:00 crush
- From Kamata: Keihin-Tōhoku to Ueno → Hibiya Line, ~45 min
- **Decision needed:** Could your wife check this out solo on her first evening if she's up for it? You land Fri 20th so it's gone by then. It only happens twice in Nov 2026 (the 7th and 19th) — no third rooster day this year

## 2. Kamata Nights — Specific Extras

We discussed Kamata as the Haneda-adjacent base but I don't know if these specifics made it into your docs:

- **Nihao** — the classic hane-tsuki gyoza spot (crispy-skirted dumplings)
- **Kamata Onsen** and **Hasunuma Onsen** — kuroyu (black hot spring water) neighbourhood sentō, good for soaking off a flight or a hangover
- The drinking quarter is dense and walkable — standing bars, yakitori, small izakaya
- **Yawaragi-mizu** — you can request water alongside sake at small bars to pace the drinking
- **Charge/otōshi** system — most small Kamata bars serve a small appetiser as a cover charge (¥300–500), it's normal not optional

## 3. Gotanda Neighbourhood — The Doorstep Stuff

The Mets Premier is confirmed, but check you have these local spots captured:

- **Track lanes (west side)** — standing bars, yakitori, small izakaya under/beside the elevated tracks. 2-min walk. This is the scruffiness you wanted from Kōenji, and it's on your doorstep every night
- **Togoshi-Ginza** — 2 stops on Ikegami Line. Tokyo's longest shopping street (1.3 km, ~400 shops). Croquettes, old sweet stalls, almost entirely locals
- **Togoshi-Ginza Onsen** — genuine kuroyu sentō, ~20 min walk or one stop from the arcade. Worth an evening
- **Musashi-Koyama** — one stop further. Palm arcade — even longer, fully covered. Good for a rainy day
- **Nakameguro / Yutenji** — a few min on Tokyu lines. Meguro River walk, vinyl and vintage shops. No specific shops were named but the area was flagged as worth an afternoon
- **Tachinomi (standing bars)** — the format is sake-led, no seats, no bookings, cheap. Gotanda track lanes have good ones

## 4. Mon 23 — Meiji Jingu Niiname-sai Details

Check you have the full photography brief:

- **Arrive 08:00–08:30** for best light and empty grounds
- The photographable elements are OUTSIDE: ~10 vegetable takarabune (treasure ships), donated sake and wine barrels, prize-winning produce displays near the south approach by the great torii
- Photography is restricted during the 10:00 ceremony in the main hall — don't build the morning around getting ceremony shots
- Chrysanthemum displays carry over from the Autumn Grand Festival
- **Gaien ginkgo illumination** from 16:30 — fits naturally between Meiji Jingu and HATO dinner in Hatsudai (~20 min from Aoyama-itchōme)

## 5. Ōmiya Bonsai Village — Logistics Detail

We went back and forth on which day this lands. Whichever day you've settled on, check these are captured:

- **Closes Thursdays** — only two days work during your Tokyo week
- **Winter hours: museum 09:00–16:00, last admission 15:30**
- Nurseries (Mansei-en, Fuyō-en, Seikō-en, Tōju-en, Kyūka-en) have split/irregular hours — check closer to date
- **Photography etiquette varies between nurseries** — some are relaxed, some don't allow photographing the trees (a photo of a masterpiece bonsai is essentially a catalogue image of a very expensive asset). Ask at each gate
- Recommended sequence: nurseries first, then museum
- Route from Gotanda: Yamanote one stop to Ōsaki → Shōnan-Shinjuku Line to Ōmiya → one stop Utsunomiya Line to Toro → 5 min walk from east exit. ~1 hour door to door

## 6. Asakusa Day — Extra Detail

Cross-check these are in your plan:

- **Kama-Asa on Kappabashi** — hand-forged Japanese knives, same steel/smith tradition as katana. Flagged as the best thing you could take home given the sword interest. Don't miss this
- **Kamiya Bar** — Japan's oldest Western-style bar (1880). Home of Denki Bran, a fierce brandy cocktail served since the Meiji era. Order it straight with a beer chaser
- **Hoppy-dōri** — strip of outdoor izakaya behind Hanayashiki: plastic stools, grilled offal, Hoppy by the jug. Good evening option
- **Asakusa Engei Hall** — rakugo (traditional comic storytelling). Alternative evening option if you're still in the area
- **River cruise** — option to cruise from Azumabashi down to Hamarikyū Gardens after the Sumida Park riverside walk

## 7. Ryōgoku Day — Extras Beyond the Core Plan

The main plan (stable practice → Sumo Museum → Kappo Yoshiba → Sword Museum → Kyū-Yasuda Garden) is solid. These extras were discussed:

- **Sumida Hokusai Museum** — striking Kazuyo Sejima building, compact collection, about an hour. Fits in the afternoon if time allows
- **Eko-in temple** — sumo's original home; tournaments were held in its grounds for roughly a century before the Kokugikan. Then a drink by the Sumida riverside as it gets dark
- **Arashio-beya** — free backup if the guided stable visit falls through. Stable in Nihonbashi with street-level windows for watching morning training. Walk-ins, no booking. Check their calendar as they're often away or closed
- **Stable booking warning:** stables set their own schedules, often confirm late, and can cancel at short notice. Book early but treat it as the one item that might not survive contact with reality. The rest of the Ryōgoku day works from 10:00 if the 07:30 falls through

## 9. Eureka! Dinner — Booking & Budget Detail

- **Marie Chiba** — Sake Samurai, co-author of "Sake Pairing"
- 12 counter seats + small standing area, Nishiazabu (2F of a converted residential building)
- 200+ sake including custom batches; she stocks Senkin, Aramasa, Raifuku
- **Signature moves:** blue-cheese ham katsu with doburoku; squid-ink oeuf mayo; sake warmed to different temps
- **Budget warning:** food is reasonable, rare sake is where it adds up. Moderate hand = ¥15,000–20,000pp; easy to sail past that chasing rare bottles
- **Getting there from Kōenji:** Chūō to Shinjuku → Toei Ōedo to Roppongi → 8 min walk. ~35 min total. Or 25–35 min taxi
- **Book via hotel concierge** or TableCheck / Pocket Concierge (tiny counter, books out, Japanese-language reservation systems)
- **Alternative:** Mizunotori in Ebisu (formerly GEM by Moto) — same leave-it-to-the-expert sake pairing format, easier to reach (~25 min from Kōenji on Yamanote)

## 11. Food & Drink Concepts — Reference

We went deep on these distinctions. Worth having as a quick reference:

| Term | What it is |
|------|-----------|
| **Izakaya** | Drinking comes first, food supports it. Order in waves, sit for hours |
| **Kappo** | Food-focused counter dining, chef cooks in front of you. Improvised around what came in that morning. Your value sweet spot |
| **Kaiseki** | Fixed multi-course procession, private tatami room, server brings dishes. Formal, seasonal, ceremonial |
| **Omakase** | "I leave it up to you" — a way of ordering, not a cuisine. Works with sushi, yakitori, tempura, anything |
| **Tachinomi** | Standing bars. Sake-led, no seats, no bookings, cheap |
| **Obanzai** | Kyoto home-style comfort food. Good for lunch or low-key early dinner |

## 12. Practical Bits We Didn't Cover

Things that may or may not be in your VS Code docs — flagging because we never discussed them:

- [ ] **IC card (Suica/Pasmo)** — buy/top up at Haneda on arrival; needed for all Tokyo transit and many vending machines and konbini. (Note: Nagiso in Kiso Valley has no IC readers — paper tickets required there)
- [ ] **Cash situation** — many small izakaya, sentō, and standing bars in Kamata/Gotanda/Kōenji are cash only. ATMs at 7-Eleven and Japan Post take international cards
- [ ] **Pocket WiFi / SIM** — did you sort this? Haneda arrivals area has rental counters
- [ ] **Rain contingency** — late November Tokyo averages ~50mm rain. Musashi-Koyama's covered Palm arcade is good for a wet afternoon. Kappabashi and indoor museums (Hokusai, Sword Museum, Sumo Museum) are all dry options
- [ ] **Konbini tips** — 7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart are your best friends for cash withdrawals, onigiri, hot drinks, and last-minute supplies
- [ ] **Scheduling buffer** — the Sun–Fri plan is solid but quite full. The arrival day (Sun 22) and Kōenji day (Thu 26) are the loosest. If something falls through or you just want to wander, those are the days to flex
