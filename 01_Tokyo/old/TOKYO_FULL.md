## What's on the doorstep

**The station lanes.** Gotanda's drinking quarter is under and beside the tracks on the west side — standing bars, yakitori, small izakaya, and absolutely nothing trying to impress you.

**Togoshi-Ginza**, two stops down the Ikegami Line. Tokyo's longest shopping street — 1.3 km of covered and open arcade, ~400 shops, croquettes and old sweet stalls, almost entirely locals. **Togoshi-Ginza Onsen** is there too: a proper neighbourhood sento with genuine black *kuroyu* hot-spring water, ~20 minutes' walk or one stop from the hotel.

**Musashi-Koyama**, one stop further — an even longer covered arcade (Palm) that stays dry whatever the weather.

**Nakameguro and Yutenji** — a few minutes on the Tokyu lines. Meguro River walk, vinyl and vintage.

**Koenji** — Yamanote to Shinjuku, then the Chuo, ~25 minutes total. An afternoon-into-evening trip: vintage shops from ~1pm, then the alleys after dark.

## Two additions for wife's interests

### Omiya Bonsai Village, Saitama

~35 minutes from central Tokyo. After the 1923 earthquake, Tokyo's bonsai growers relocated here as a group and their nurseries are still working — five or six of them, free to walk into, on quiet suburban streets. The **Omiya Bonsai Art Museum** anchors it with a curated collection and English signage. Half a day. **Closed Thursdays.**

### The Japanese Sword Museum (NBTHK), Ryogoku

The national institution for *nihonto*, with rotating displays of masterpiece blades — mostly Important Cultural Properties and National Treasures. Serious museum, not a tourist attraction: about the steel, the grain, the *hamon*. Scheduled for the Ryogoku day (Wed 25).

## Sumo — what's possible

**No tournament in Tokyo during the trip.** The November basho is the Kyushu Basho in Fukuoka (8–22 Nov). The Kokugikan will be dark.

**Options:**

1. **Morning practice (*asa-geiko*) at a Ryogoku stable.** The best substitute. Only possible after 22 November (wrestlers return from Fukuoka). Window is **24–27 Nov**. Book through a guide or agency — most stables won't admit unaccompanied foreign visitors. Scheduled for Wed 25.

2. **Asakusa Sumo Club** — sumo show with retired wrestlers, chanko-nabe, geisha dance, and a shot at getting in the ring. ~US$110pp. **Book ahead, they sell out.** Scheduled for Tue 24 at 12:00.

3. **Arashio-beya** (Nihonbashi) — free, watch morning training through street-level windows, walk-ins accepted. Good backup if the guided stable visit falls through. Check their calendar.

## Meiji Jingu + local day — Monday 23 November *(Labour Thanksgiving Day)*

### Morning — Meiji Jingu Niinamesai + street photography

November 23 is **Labour Thanksgiving Day** (*Kinro Kansha no Hi*), the modern name for the ancient **Niinamesai** harvest thanksgiving. Meiji Jingu holds the ceremony at **10:00 am**: fresh crops are presented to the kami, and roughly ten *takarabune* (treasure ships) made from vegetables are displayed around the shrine buildings. Chrysanthemums line the main approach, left over from the Autumn Grand Festival.

It's a public holiday so the shrine and surrounding park will be buzzing — good energy for street photography.

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| ~07:00 | Leave Gotanda. Yamanote to Yoyogi Station (~15 min). |
| 07:15 | Enter from the **north (Yoyogi) entrance**. Walk south through the forest approach — towering camphor trees, gravel path, morning light filtering through. Shoot as you go. |
| ~08:00 | Arrive at the main shrine complex. The shrine opens at sunrise (~06:20 in late Nov) so it'll be active. Explore the grounds — the torii, the sake barrel wall, the inner garden approach. |
| 10:00 | **Niinamesai ceremony** begins. The vegetable treasure ships and crop displays will be set up around the shrine building. Observe and photograph. |
| ~11:00 | Walk south through **Yoyogi Park** — Harajuku side. On a holiday this will be full of life: buskers, dancers, picnickers, dog walkers. More street photography. |
| ~12:00 | Exit via Harajuku / Omotesando. Coffee and lunch. |

**Confirm the ceremony schedule on meijijingu.or.jp closer to the date** — time is consistently listed as 10:00 but worth verifying for 2026.

## Ryogoku day — Wednesday 25 November

Chosen deliberately. Monday 23 is a holiday so most museums push Monday closure to Tuesday 24 — Wednesday dodges that. Weekday, so the free Sumo Museum inside the Kokugikan is open (shuts weekends). After the basho, so morning practice is live.

**Edo-Tokyo Museum reopened 31 March 2026** after a four-year renovation — Ryogoku's biggest draw is back. Needs 2+ hours of its own. Either swap it in for the Hokusai museum, or give it a separate half-day.

| Time | Where |
|---|---|
| 07:30 | **Sumo morning practice** at a stable. Silence, no photos unless permitted. Book ahead through a guide. |
| 10:00 | **Ryogoku Kokugikan** — the exterior, wrestler handprint plaques, and the free **Sumo Museum** inside. |
| 11:45 | **Chanko-nabe lunch.** *Kappo Yoshiba* occupies a former stable with the original *dohyo* still in the dining room. *Chanko Tomoegata* is the solid alternative. |
| 13:30 | **The Japanese Sword Museum (NBTHK).** Ninety minutes, properly. Closed Mondays; open today. |
| 15:00 | **Kyu-Yasuda Garden** — free, beside the sword museum. Small Edo daimyo garden with a tidal pond that once rose and fell with the Sumida. |
| 15:45 | **Sumida Hokusai Museum.** Hokusai was born in these streets. Compact, in a striking Kazuyo Sejima building. |
| 17:15 | **Eko-in** — sumo's original home, where tournaments were held for roughly a century before the Kokugikan existed. Then a drink by the river as it gets dark. |

**Getting there:** Gotanda → Yamanote to Akihabara → Sobu Line, ~30 minutes. For 07:30 practice start, leave ~06:45 — the one early morning of the Tokyo leg.
