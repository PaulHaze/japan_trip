# Sat 21 Nov — Kamata decompression

**Deliberately unplanned.** Both in Tokyo after staggered long-haul arrivals. Low ambition.

### Easy options (pick what you feel like)

**Kamata wandering.** The JR Kamata east exit area is good scruffy izakaya territory — yakitori, gyoza, ramen, standing bars. Wander and eat.

**Ikegami Honmonji.** Large historic Buddhist temple complex in Ota, 10 minutes by train. Temple grounds, cemetery, old neighbourhood streets, quieter Tokyo. A good "we're already in this part of the city" option that doesn't require crossing Tokyo.

**Kamata / Hasunuma Onsen.** Proper black *kuroyu* bathhouses within walking distance. Good jet-lag recovery.

**Wife's spa.** Previously researched options near Tosei: Shu. Beauty & Relax, Thai Leela, Monsiam, KOKOCHI.spa, Grand Raffine. Re-check availability.

### Alternative if you're feeling more ambitious

**Oi Racecourse Flea Market** — running Sat 21, Sun 22, Mon 23. Roughly 08:45–14:15 (weather dependent). One of Tokyo's biggest rough-and-ready flea markets: second-hand clothes, cameras, watches, household oddities, treasure hunting. Not curated antiques, just good rummaging. Don't force it on a recovery day, but it's there.

# Mon 23 Nov — Meiji Jingu + local *(Labour Thanksgiving Day)*

A public holiday. Central tourist districts will be heaving — the plan deliberately avoids them.

### Morning — Meiji Jingu Niinamesai + street photography 📷

November 23 is the modern name for the ancient **Niinamesai** harvest thanksgiving. Meiji Jingu holds the ceremony at **10:00**: fresh crops presented to the kami, roughly ten *takarabune* (treasure ships) made from vegetables displayed around the shrine buildings. Chrysanthemums line the main approach from the earlier Autumn Grand Festival.

Public holiday + shrine festival = buzzing grounds. Good street photography energy.

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| ~07:00 | Leave Gotanda. Yamanote to **Yoyogi Station** (~15 min). |
| 07:15 | Enter from the **north (Yoyogi) entrance**. Walk south through the forest approach — towering camphor trees, gravel path, morning light through the canopy. Shoot as you go. |
| ~08:00 | Main shrine complex. Explore — the torii, sake barrel wall, inner garden approach. |
| 10:00 | **Niinamesai ceremony** begins. Vegetable treasure ships and crop displays around the shrine building. |
| ~11:00 | Walk south through **Yoyogi Park** — on a holiday it'll be full of life: buskers, dancers, performers, dog walkers. Street photography gold. |
| ~12:00 | Exit via Harajuku. Coffee and lunch. |

**Confirm ceremony schedule on meijijingu.or.jp closer to the date.**

### Optional: Harajuku cat cafe

SOL's notes say your wife wants to visit a cat cafe around Harajuku after Meiji Jingu. Slot it in around 11:30–12:00 before lunch. Pick and book closer to the date.

### Afternoon — choose one

**Option A — Togoshi-Ginza + onsen (local, easy).** Back to the Gotanda neighbourhood. Togoshi-Ginza arcade (Tokyo's longest shopping street, croquettes, old sweet stalls), Musashi-Koyama Palm arcade, then **Togoshi-Ginza Onsen** for the black *kuroyu* soak. Good holiday-afternoon energy in the arcades.

**Option B — Oedo Antique Market (if the antique itch is strong).** Running this same day, 09:00–16:00, Tokyo International Forum, Yurakucho. ~250 dealers, free entry, cancelled in rain. Japanese antiques, ceramics, textiles, kimono, prints, watches, old toys, ephemera. Extremely good fit for your interests — but it means crossing Tokyo rather than staying local. Don't try to do both this and Togoshi; pick one. If you go, skip the morning shrine and go to the market at opening, then do Meiji Jingu in the afternoon when the market closes — or accept that the market is the day and Meiji Jingu is the morning.

### Evening — HATO in Hatsudai

**HATO** — verify if booked; book now if not.

# Tue 24 Nov — Asakusa full day 🏯

**Tuesday, not Sunday or Monday.** Kappabashi shuts Sundays, and the 23rd is a holiday when Asakusa is genuinely unpleasant. Tuesday is a normal weekday and everything's open.

From Gotanda: Asakusa Line direct, no changes, ~25 minutes.

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 08:00 | **Senso-ji early.** Kaminarimon, near-empty Nakamise, main hall with incense. A completely different place from the one at eleven. |
| 09:00 | **Asakusa Shrine** — 1649, survived both the earthquake and the war. Then **Denboin-dori**, the quiet side street. |
| 09:45 | Coffee and breakfast. |
| 10:15 | **Kappabashi** — 800 m of kitchenware and restaurant supply. **Kama-Asa** for hand-forged Japanese knives (same steel tradition as the katana). Plastic food sample shops for fun. |
| 12:00 | **Asakusa Sumo Club** — retired-wrestler sumo show, all-you-can-eat chanko-nabe, geisha dance. Two hours. ~US$110pp. **Book ahead, they sell out.** |
| 14:30 | **Sumida Park and the river** — Asahi building, Skytree across the water, embankment walk. Or a water bus cruise down to Hamarikyu from Azumabashi. |
| 16:30 | **Kamiya Bar** — Japan's oldest Western-style bar, 1880. Home of Denki Bran. Straight, with a beer chaser. |
| 18:00 | **Hoppy-dori** — outdoor izakaya behind Hanayashiki: plastic stools, grilled offal, Hoppy by the jug. Or **Asakusa Engei Hall** for rakugo. |

### If you're not feeling Asakusa

**Yanesen (Yanaka + Sendagi + Nezu).** Treat all three as one continuous walking area. Start at Nippori, walk through Yanaka Cemetery, temple streets, Yanaka Ginza, Sendagi backstreets, end at Nezu Shrine. Older low-rise Tokyo, small temples, wooden houses, traditional shops. Don't just do Yanaka Ginza and leave — the depth is in the backstreets. Kappabashi can be done separately another morning.

**Shibamata.** "Tokyo from another era." Old approach shopping street, Shibamata Taishakuten temple, traditional snacks, Yamamoto-tei historic house, Edo River walk. Completely self-contained.

# Wed 25 Nov — Nakano → Koenji → Shinjuku late night 🌃

The big day-into-night outing. All three are on or near the Chuo Line — the geography flows naturally.

**Getting there:** Yamanote to Shinjuku (~8 min), Chuo Line to Nakano (one stop, ~4 min).

### Late morning / early afternoon — Nakano

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| ~10:30 | Arrive Nakano. |
| 10:30–14:00 | **Nakano Broadway.** The treasure-hunting complex. Multiple floors of collectibles, watches, old toys, books, vintage cameras, niche everything. **Jack Road** and **Bee Knee** for watches specifically — Nakano Broadway is one of Tokyo's best spots for second-hand and vintage timepieces. The **Sun Mall** covered shotengai leads from the station to Broadway. Take your time, this is the main event. |
| ~14:00 | Lunch in Nakano — good ramen and izakaya around the station. |

### Late afternoon / evening — Koenji

One stop west on the Chuo Line (~2 min). Arrive around 15:00.

Koenji was originally considered as the Tokyo base because it matches your style so well. The afternoon-into-evening window is when it's at its best.

- **Vintage clothing** — Koenji is Tokyo's best suburb for this, multiple shops on every block
- **Record shops** — vinyl and CDs, the good kind of digging
- **Shotengai** — old covered shopping streets with local character
- **Kissaten** — old-school coffee shops, wood-panelled, no wifi
- **Small live-music venues** — punk and alternative history

Wander rather than chase addresses. The suburb itself is the activity. Stay until the tiny bars and alleys light up after dark.

### Late night — Shinjuku

Chuo Line back to Shinjuku (~5 min from Koenji). Arrive 20:00–21:00ish.

This is the neon-and-alleyways session.

**Omoide Yokocho** (Piss Alley) — immediately beside Shinjuku Station's west exit. Tiny yakitori stalls in narrow lanes, smoke, lanterns, elbow-to-elbow. Have a skewer and a beer even if you've eaten.

**Kabukicho.** Walk east through the station. The neon canyons, the touts, the Godzilla head on the cinema. Seedy-spectacular Tokyo. The **Kabukicho Tower** (opened 2023) sits in the middle of it.

**Golden Gai.** Six lanes, roughly 200 bars, most seating four to eight people. Not all welcome walk-ins — look for signs in English or an open door. Cover charge (usually ¥500–1,000) is normal. Have two or three bars, not one.

**Hanazono Shrine** — right next to Golden Gai, lit up and atmospheric at night. A breather between bars.

**Late train home:** last Yamanote to Gotanda is around **00:30**. If you miss it, a taxi is ~¥2,500–3,000, or ride it out until the first train at ~04:30 (this is Shinjuku — there are 4am options).

### If the Shinjuku late night doesn't happen

Maybe you're flagging by Koenji. That's fine — Koenji's own tiny bars are a perfectly good evening. Head back when you're done.

**Alternative Wednesday: Kichijoji.** If Nakano doesn't grab you, Kichijoji (two more stops on the Chuo) is one of Tokyo's best all-in-one suburb days: independent shops, backstreets, Inokashira Park autumn colour, Benzaiten shrine, Harmonica Yokocho for dinner/drinks.

# Thu 26 Nov — Ryogoku: sumo, swords + Eureka! farewell ⚔️

Moved from Wednesday to make room for Nakano/Koenji/Shinjuku. Everything still works: weekday (Sumo Museum open, Sword Museum open, no holiday closures), and it's within the sumo-practice window (24–27 Nov).

**Getting there:** Gotanda → Yamanote to Akihabara → Sobu Line to Ryogoku, ~30 min. For 07:30 practice, leave ~06:45.

| Time | Where |
|---|---|
| 07:30 | **Sumo morning practice (*asa-geiko*)** at a stable. Silence, no photos unless permitted. Book through a guide/agency. |
| 10:00 | **Ryogoku Kokugikan** — the exterior, wrestler handprint plaques, free **Sumo Museum** inside. |
| 11:45 | **Chanko-nabe lunch.** *Kappo Yoshiba* — former stable, original *dohyo* in the dining room. *Chanko Tomoegata* as alternative. |
| 13:30 | **The Japanese Sword Museum (NBTHK).** Ninety minutes, properly. Masterpiece blades, grain patterns, *hamon*. |
| 15:00 | **Kyu-Yasuda Garden** — free, beside the sword museum. Small Edo daimyo garden. |
| 15:30 | **Choose one:** the **Sumida Hokusai Museum** (compact, Kazuyo Sejima building) or the **Edo-Tokyo Museum** (reopened March 2026, the bigger draw, needs 2+ hours). Don't try both unless you're museum-hungry. |
| ~17:00 | **Eko-in** — sumo's original home. A drink by the river as it gets dark. |

### Evening — Eureka! farewell dinner 🥂

Back to Gotanda to change, then **Eureka!** in Nishiazabu for the sake-pairing dinner. Last proper night before Matsumoto. **Book now if not already done.**

### If sumo practice falls through

Stables confirm late and the booking isn't guaranteed.

- **Arashio-beya** (Nihonbashi) — free backup, watch training through street-level windows.
- **Sleep in** and start at 10:00 instead. You'll deserve the lie-in after Shinjuku the night before.
- Fill the morning with **Kiyosumi-Shirakawa** — gardens, coffee, converted warehouses. Two stops from Ryogoku on the Oedo Line.

### Alternative Thursday (if Ryogoku doesn't appeal)

**Jindaiji + Chofu.** Ancient Buddhist temple in wooded surroundings, soba restaurants, traditional lanes, daruma shops, Jindai Botanical Gardens. One of the strongest self-contained neighbourhood days. The sword museum can be done separately.

**Nishi-Ogikubo.** Possibly the strongest "wander and find weird old stuff" suburb in Tokyo. Antiques, second-hand books, old kissaten, kimono, eccentric shops, small izakaya. No major sight, the suburb is the activity.

### Booking the Azusa — step by step

**Every seat is reserved. There are no unreserved cars.** This is a Friday in peak autumn-colour season — the Azusa sells out a week or more ahead. Book as early as possible.

**How to book:**

1. **Eki-net** (JR East's online reservation system) — the easiest way.
   - Create a free account at the [eki-net English site](https://www.eki-net.com/en/)
   - Search: Shinjuku → Matsumoto, Fri 27 Nov, 10:00
   - Pay by credit card
   - You'll get a **QR code** — as of November 2026, JR East's Q Ticket system should be fully rolled out, meaning you can go through the gates at Shinjuku by scanning the QR on your phone. No paper ticket needed.
   - **If QR gates aren't working for your service**, collect paper tickets at a JR East ticket machine (green machines with the "eki-net" logo) — scan the QR code at the machine, confirm, and it prints your tickets.

2. **JR East ticket machines** — the green "Midori-no-Madoguchi" machines at any JR station. Tickets go on sale one month before travel (so from **27 October** for your train). You can also buy at the JR East Travel Service Centre at Shinjuku.

3. **Don't buy on the train.** You can technically buy from the conductor, but it costs an extra ¥260 and you won't get a seat assignment.

**Fare:** ~¥5,720 per person (¥3,740 base + ¥1,980 limited express, as of March 2026 pricing). Confirm when booking — fares may adjust.

**Sit on the left-hand side.** Mt Fuji shows up around Otsuki and across the Kofu basin. Later the Southern Alps come up on the same side approaching Kobuchizawa. In late November with clear winter air, it's a genuinely good ride.

### Takkyubin (luggage forwarding) — step by step

You're sending the big suitcases directly from Gotanda to your Kyoto hotel so you travel light through Matsumoto and Narai.

**Before the trip:**
- **Email the Kyoto hotel now** and confirm they will accept and hold luggage arriving **Saturday 28 November** (one day before your check-in on the 29th). Get a yes in writing, especially if it's a small property. Give them your name and check-in date.

**At the Gotanda hotel (Thursday evening or Friday morning):**

1. Tell the front desk: *"takkyubin onegaishimasu"* (宅急便お願いします). They handle this routinely.
2. They'll give you a **Yamato waybill slip** (the *denpyo* — a multi-part form). Fill in:
   - **Sender:** your name, Gotanda hotel address, your phone number
   - **Recipient:** your name, **Kyoto hotel name and full address**, your phone number
   - **Requested delivery date:** write **Saturday 28 November** or **Sunday 29 November** (your Kyoto check-in day) — whichever the Kyoto hotel prefers
   - **Contents:** clothing / personal items (*irui* 衣類)
3. The desk will weigh and measure the bags and calculate the cost.
4. **Pay at the desk.** Most hotels accept cash or card for this — confirm with Gotanda when you check in. Carry cash as backup.
5. Yamato picks up from the hotel. **Tokyo to Kyoto is next-day delivery** on the standard service.

**Cost:** ~¥2,500–2,600 per standard suitcase (Yamato size 140–160). Budget **~¥5,000–5,200 for the pair.**

**What you travel with to Matsumoto:**
- One large cabin bag each
- Daypacks
- **All cold-weather gear** — coats, thermals, gloves, proper shoes. This stays with you, not in the shipped bags.
